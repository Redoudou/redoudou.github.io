01 — Objective
This is not intended to become a complicated craft-cocktail bar. The approach is to preserve the familiar drinks guests expect, remove bottles that are redundant or unlikely to be poured, and redirect that value toward a smaller number of versatile ingredients.
The upgraded inventory supports the couple's signature cocktails, a zero-proof option, a late-night Irish coffee service and a broader range of classic drinks — while staying practical for high-volume wedding service.
The guiding principle is simple: fewer redundant bottles, better ingredients and greater flexibility behind the bar.

06 — Campari
Why Campari matters
Campari is one of the highest-utility substitutions in the bar. With one ingredient, the bar can make Negronis, Americanos, Boulevardiers, Sbagliatos, Campari sodas and rum-and-grapefruit drinks.
It works across gin, whiskey, rum, mezcal and sparkling-wine formats — which is why replacing Amaretto with Campari materially improves the bar without adding complexity.

09 — Economics
Budget and net effect
The complete venue bar tab is expected to be approximately $10,000, including product cost, venue markup and service structure. Across 250 guests, that is approximately $40 per guest.
The relevant question is not the gross retail price of the proposed bottles. It is the net cost of substituting them for bottles already included in the premium package.
The specialty-inventory value for the chosen scenario is calculated live in section 08 — change the servings there and the bottle count and retail-equivalent figure below the selector update with it.
The retail-equivalent value of the specialty cocktail inventory is not necessarily an additional charge on top of the existing bar package.
Several products — Bulleit, Jameson and other core spirits — are already included in the premium package, and the proposed bottles replace Captain Morgan, Cuervo Gold, Crown Royal Apple, Malibu, Kahlúa and Amaretto. The venue should calculate the net substitution and volume cost, not add the full retail-equivalent total to the existing ~$10,000 bar bill.
What this estimate covers
Approximately 600 cocktails only. Beer, wine, sparkling wine and standard mixed drinks made from the venue's existing vodka, gin and tequila are not included.
The exact bottle mix may shift with guest preference — the venue should be allowed to open reserve bottles progressively rather than the entire allocation at the start of service.
